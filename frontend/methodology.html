Data, methodology & privacy

How Lilikas na ga? works, where the numbers come from, and what it does with your information.

Planning aid, not an official warning service. Hazard results, routes and alerts help you prepare. Always follow PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, the Batangas City CDRRMO and your barangay. In an emergency call 911.

Hazard levels

Flood and landslide classes come from the mapped susceptibility zones at your point. Fault proximity is the distance to the nearest mapped active fault (High under 1 km, Moderate under 5 km, Low under 15 km). The fault lines were traced from map images (about ±50 m), so these tiers are a planning heuristic, not an official classification.

The Overview risk level

The level combines what is mapped at your place (flood and landslide susceptibility) with what is happening now: rain in the last 3 hours from Open-Meteo (30 mm or more counts as heavy), active rainfall advisories, storms forecast to pass near, and recent nearby earthquakes.

  • High risk · Prepare: a high mapped hazard plus heavy rain, an advisory or a nearby storm.
  • Moderate risk · Stay alert: a high mapped hazard, or current weather or earthquake activity.
  • Low risk · Stay informed: neither.

“High susceptibility” does not mean it is flooding, and the level is never an evacuation order.

Best center and routes

Each open center is scored in minutes (lower is better): travel time, plus a penalty when it is more than 60% full, plus penalties for high or medium hazards at the center and for the share of the road that crosses high flood or landslide zones. Closed, unavailable and full centers are never recommended. Road geometry comes from the public OpenStreetMap routing service; Drive reuses the walking route with an estimated speed. If routing is unreachable, straight-line estimates are shown and labelled. Evacuation center data is sample data and capacity is simulated until official records are connected.

Alerts

Earthquakes come from PHIVOLCS (via the phivocs-api service), storms from the Bagyo API (JMA, JTWC, NHC data), and rain/flood or local advisories are posted by staff. Each source is checked every few minutes. If a source is down, the Alerts page says so instead of failing silently.

How Lilikas Assist uses AI

  • The AI only orders guides from a fixed library and fills in a report draft from what you said. It never writes new safety steps and never issues orders.
  • Everything the AI returns is checked against fixed lists. Emergency words (“nalulunod”, “trapped”, …) are caught by fixed rules, not left to the AI.
  • The “Why this risk level?” explanation may be rephrased by the AI, but it is rejected if it reads like an order or adds numbers that were not in the evidence.
  • If the AI is off or not responding, simple rules do the same jobs, so Assist always works.
  • Guides are drafts until reviewed by the Batangas City CDRRMO, and are labelled that way.

Privacy & location permissions

  • Your selected area, checklist, family contacts and alert choices are stored on this device (browser storage). If you sign in, your area, alert topics and checklist are also saved to your account.
  • Device location is used only when you choose “Use my location”, and your browser asks first. It is not continuous tracking and is not shared with anyone.
  • A report you send through Assist contains the report details, your approximate location and what you said. It is stored for Batangas City staff to read. Family contacts never leave your device; texting them happens in your own messaging app.
  • Voice recognition uses your browser’s speech service. In Chrome and Edge the audio may be processed by Google or Microsoft.

Terms

This service is provided as is, as a preparedness aid. It does not dispatch responders or monitor requests for help, and delivery of alerts or notifications is not guaranteed.
